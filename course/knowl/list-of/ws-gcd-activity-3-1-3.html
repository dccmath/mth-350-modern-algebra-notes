Definition 16 Greatest common divisor.

Let \(a\) and \(b\) be integers, not both zero. A common divisor of \(a\) and \(b\) is any integer \(k\) such that \(k\mid a\) and \(k\mid b\text{.}\) We say that an integer \(d\) is the greatest common divisor of \(a\) and \(b\text{,}\) denoted \(\gcd(a,b)\text{,}\) provided that \(d\) is a common divisor of \(a\) and \(b\text{,}\) and if \(k\) is a common divisor of \(a\) and \(b\) then \(k\leq d\text{.}\)
